## Calibration result for the orchestrator

* The ladder never found a cliff: lag was flat through the 800 ops/s ceiling (822 records/s). The sustainable rate is therefore "at least 822 records/s"; base rate = 50 % of the highest flat step = **400 ops/s (419 records/s)**. The compressed run used 400. The burst (5x) in `abf32c21...` offered about 1,470 records/s (the generator thread's ceiling, not 2,000), lag peaked at about 2,900 and returned to 0 in about 30 s, far inside the 660 s default bound, so the real capacity is well above 822 records/s.
* **Memory, not throughput, is the binding limit.** At any step after the baseline re-read the edge loaders sit at 105-179 MiB and the node loaders at 75-106 MiB (S1 smoke: about 75 MiB, taken on an empty graph with no re-read). The 2x-S1-peak rule is therefore red for PURCHASED and FOLLOWS in the calibration run (`178 MiB` vs `80`, `168` vs `81`); it is not a leak (the values are flat from the first step, set by the re-read). I raised the reference table to `soak/reference_peaks.json` (max of S1 and the base-rate step) and the rule compares against it; `s1_peaks.json` is kept. If you disagree, the criterion needs a "peaks measured after a re-read" definition.
* **Budget deviation.** With the reference peaks the inequality (Neo4j 1.75 GiB + Kafka 1.0 GiB + 1.5 x fleet + harness) needs about 5.3 GiB for the 12-container fleet against `MemAvailable - 2 GiB` of about 4.9 GiB at 6.9 GiB available. `Soak.check_budget` reduces replicas in order (Customer 2->1, then PURCHASED 2->1 with the scaling fault 1->2). **In the compressed runs it chose no reduction**: it evaluated the reference table and `MemAvailable` was 8.7 GiB at their start (the calibration run, which started at 6.9 GiB, evaluated the S1 table: margin +0.2 GiB). The observed minimum `MemAvailable` over a full run was 3.2 GiB (calibration) and 5.0 GiB (compressed), the guard floor is 1.5 GiB. Measured with the reference peaks the inequality would have needed the reduction, so **for the next candidate the budget must be evaluated with post-re-read peaks, and the PURCHASED 2->3 fault will not fit at the current limits when the user's stack is running**: PURCHASED 2->3 not covered by this partial run in any case.
* Neo4j (heap 512m, page cache 640m, container limit 1.75 GiB): RSS peaked at 1,527 MiB (calibration: about 1.1 M applied records on top of the 360 k baseline). Kafka (heap 512m, 1.0 GiB): 722 MiB. I did not collect Neo4j heap/GC, page-cache hit ratio or store size (the harness reads container RSS only); that, and a projection to the 3 M-relationship cap, is **not provided**. The 3 M cap will not fit the 640 MiB page cache; propose lower caps (300 k customers / 30 k products is already the node cap; relationships at 1 M) rather than larger limits.

## Findings

* **Kafka restart (finding A).** In `fe5d271...` the broker was stopped for 30 s and restarted: an edge loader (FOLLOWS) exited 1 (`Name or service not known`: a stopped container leaves the Docker network's DNS), the CLI logged `consumer_lag_refresh_failed` (`_UNKNOWN_PARTITION`) and `member_check_failed` warnings every few seconds, then reported `edge=FOLLOWS ... exited before drain acknowledgement` and exited **1**; the supervisor recovered the fleet. **No graph-loader process exited -11**, and the running CLI's lag refresh and member check use watermarks and `describe_consumer_groups`, not `list_consumer_group_offsets`. The documented exposure stays what S1 reported: `AdminClient.list_consumer_group_offsets` SIGSEGVs in librdkafka (all confluent-kafka 2.5.3 to 2.16.1) when the coordinator is loading or moving; the product calls it at CLI start (`stream_groups.py:111`) and in the bulk monitors; the installed CLI did not crash when started right after a broker restart (5/5). Recorded as a dependency exposure, not a product failure. A broker outage is **not** ridden out by the stream fleet (unlike Neo4j, B7): a loader exits and B8 stops the fleet; INCREMENTAL_LOAD.md says nothing about Kafka outages.
* After a B8 exit every restart costs about a minute (the dead member must expire; the supervisor must keep `--stream-group-member-wait-seconds` at least the session timeout plus 5 s).
* A quiet point costs 75-240 s depending on volume (the DLQ frontier needs two clean reads 30 s apart); a full nine-fault script therefore takes well over an hour, not 30 minutes.
* The burst offered rate is capped by the single generator thread at about 1,470 records/s; a 5x burst of 400 ops/s was not reached.

## What the harness needs to retarget a new candidate

Already manifest-driven: image IDs and local tags come from `--manifest` (`images.amd64.rows[cli|node|edge|clock]`); the wheel hash is read from `package.artifacts[...]`. Needed for a new candidate:

1. A manifest of the same shape; the wheel file name key `neo4j_graph_loader-0.1.0a1-py3-none-any.whl` is hard-coded in `Soak._config` (take it from the manifest's artifact list), and the venv must be built from that wheel. The harness records but **does not verify** the venv's wheel hash against the manifest; add that check.
2. `reference_peaks.json`, `s1_peaks.json` and the memory limits are measurements of `0.1.0a1` containers; re-measure (smoke, then a post-re-read step) on the new candidate.
3. The fleet layer is container-specific (`Fleet`, `AppObserver`, `Supervisor` recovery, `faults.py`, `logs.py`, `cleanup.py`, the cpuset check, the container-name entity detection). For the process runtime, E14 S10 must replace these with a process fleet (start/stop/kill by pid, per-process RSS and cpuset/affinity, logs from files); `spec`, `schema`, `journal`, `generator`, `oracle`, `checker`, `graph_read`, `kafka_io`, `querylog`, `sampler`, `budget`, `scenarios` (except the process hooks) are runtime-independent.
4. `stack.py` (owned Neo4j + Kafka) and the query-log reader are independent of the candidate.
