## Workload design

**Schema** (`soak/schema.yaml`, stream rules; `loading.consumer_group_id: e13-<token>`). Every type declares a string
property `rid`, the generator's unique record id, so the harness can find any record in Kafka and trace lineage without
reading Neo4j. Datetimes are sent timezone-aware (`+00:00`) and compared as UTC instants; dates (`FOLLOWS.since`) are
compared as ISO calendar dates.

| Element | Topic(s), partitions | Conflict rule | Replicas | What it exercises |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Customer` (customer_id, rid, name, tier, city, updated_at) | `customers`, 6 | - (last in partition order) | 2 | node updates, null-never-clears |
| `Product` (product_id, rid, title, price float, category) | `products`, 3 | - | 1 | node updates |
| `Store` (store_id, rid, name) | `stores`, 1 | - | 1 | tiny label |
| `PURCHASED` Customer->Product (rid, qty, amount, version int, at) | `purchases-web` 6, `purchases-app` 3 | `order_by: version` | 2 (3 after the scaling fault) | multi-topic, out-of-order versions, DLQ, late-endpoint cohort |
| `STOCKS` Store->Product (rid, quantity, updated_at) | `stock`, 6 | `order_by: updated_at` | 1 | hot keys (20 stores keyed by store) |
| `FOLLOWS` Customer->Customer (rid, since date, strength int) | `follows`, 3 | none (parked-pair index) | 1 | non-`order_by` ordering, self-reference |
| `VIEWED` Customer->Product and Customer->Store (rid, seen_at, device) | `views-product` 3, `views-store` 1 | `order_by: seen_at` | 1 | multi-pair type |

Containers: 9 loaders (Customer 2, Product 1, Store 1, PURCHASED 2, STOCKS 1, FOLLOWS 1, VIEWED 1), the retry consumer
and the lease server: 11, plus the CLI.
Every relationship type has `dead_letter.enabled: true` and `max_retry_attempts` giving a retry window of about 5 minutes
of attempts (not wall time: attempts do not advance during a system-wide outage).

**Generator** (`soak/generator.py`): seeded and deterministic; `acks=all`, idempotence on, records keyed by node key /
source key per the producer contract. Two identities: the **logical record** `rid` (a declared property; exact
duplicates reuse it with the identical payload) and the **physical send** id, unique per enqueue, carried in a Kafka
header `e13-send` (headers are not record properties). Every physical send is journalled in one of three states:
- **never enqueued** (the produce call raised): not in Kafka;
- **acknowledged**: topic, partition and offset recorded;
- **uncertain** (enqueued, then a delivery error or timeout): a header match in a read-back of the partition resolves it
  to that offset. A non-match does **not** prove absence (an append can land after the read); the send stays uncertain
  and is re-read at every later quiet point. A send still uncertain makes the comparison of the keys it touches
  **inconclusive** (reported, never counted as a product write).
The model folds every physical offset (an exact resend at a later offset matters for node and `FOLLOWS` PATCH order);
terminal accounting deduplicates by `rid`. DLQ envelopes and `replay-dlq` do not carry Kafka headers
(dead_letter.py, dead_letter_replay.py), so a replayed record's physical identity is its destination
topic/partition/offset, found by `rid` in the read-back of the range written during the replay.

Traffic mix (one config; defaults):
- Base rate set by calibration (S2): 50% of the measured sustainable throughput. Bursts of 5x base for 3 minutes.
- 35% new nodes, 15% node updates (some with optional properties null), 35% new relationships, 15% relationship updates.
- Relationship endpoints drawn Zipf (s = 1.1) over customers; 20 stores carry all `STOCKS` traffic.
- 10% of new relationships are sent before their endpoints; the endpoint is published after 1-120 s. Publishing does
  not guarantee the node is durable inside the retry window (node backlog during a burst or outage can delay it), so an
  ordinary record may still park; that is handled below as a capacity outcome, not a missing write.
- 2% exact duplicates (a resend with the same `rid` and the identical payload). 5% of `order_by` updates send an older
  value after a newer one (on the other topic for `PURCHASED`).
- 0.1% malformed records (a missing required property or a wrong type) on node and relationship topics.
- Determinism rules: an `order_by` value is reused for one pair only by an exact duplicate (same `rid`, same payload); a
  fresh `rid` always gets a fresh value. `order_by` types always send every declared property non-null. A relationship
  identity includes both endpoint labels (multi-pair `VIEWED`).
- `FOLLOWS` payload classes, fixed at generation time and immutable: **null-patch pairs** connect only baseline customers
  (bulk-loaded and durable before the stream starts, so they never wait for an endpoint) and their whole lifetime is
  scheduled inside one calm window; they may send optional nulls. Every calm window carries a deterministic, nonempty
  null-patch cohort (default 200 pairs) in which each pair sends a non-null value and then an explicit null patch for it,
  completed before the window's closing quiet point. **Complete pairs** (all others) always send every declared property
  non-null. A pair never changes class.

**Cohorts.** Records are tagged with the window they were produced in. A **calm window** is a span with no fault, restart
or replay that ends in a quiet point before the next fault. The strict claim for a null-patch pair needs only its own
preconditions: its window had no fault, restart or replay, and none of its own records entered the DLQ (checked by `rid`
in the DLQ topics). This is decided from the journal, the fault log, the supervisor's restart log and the DLQ read-back
**before** the graph is read; parking of unrelated records does not affect it, and a failed graph comparison never
disqualifies its own pair. A null-patch pair that loses its own preconditions is **inconclusive** (counted and
reported), never re-checked under the complete-pair rule.

**Controlled late-endpoint cohort** (once in S2, three times in S3, each in a calm window with the scaling and kill faults
excluded): send N = 200 new `PURCHASED` pairs (new customers, no later updates to those pairs) and withhold their
customers. Barrier: wait (deadline = retry window x 3) until the parked topic holds all N `rid`s, deduplicated by `rid`.
Then send the customers, pause the generator, run a reconciliation checkpoint (below) for `PURCHASED`, and journal the
republished records by reading the source topics' new ranges back (lineage by `rid`). Every cohort identity must then
hold its record. Resume.

**Expected-graph model** (`soak/oracle.py`), computed only from the journal (generator sends plus replay read-backs),
never from Neo4j. A parked receipt does not prove a record stayed unapplied (a park can publish, its index tombstone
fail, and a restarted retry consumer then write the original once the endpoint exists; resurrection memory is per
process), and a replay acknowledgement does not prove a record was applied (it can park again). So parking is never
modelled as "absent":
- **Intermediate quiet points:** every identity touched by a `rid` that has a parked copy is **pending** and excluded from
  that comparison (neither asserted present nor absent), and reported with its cause (burst, outage, node backlog).
- **Reconciliation checkpoints** for a set of types T (T = `PURCHASED` after each late-endpoint cohort; T = every type at
  the end of every run), with the generator paused throughout: all endpoints are sent and the node groups drained;
  capture the parked topics' end offsets **before the first replay invocation**; run `replay-dlq --parked` for each type
  in T (`replay-dlq` takes one type per call); capture fresh source end offsets, journal the replayed ranges, and drain
  again (sources and the moving DLQ frontier); require that no parked copy was added after the captured boundary, from
  the first replay through the final drain (including copies parked while the replay command was still running). Only
  then compare: identities of types in T lose their pending exclusion and are checked against the full fold of their
  physical offsets; identities of other types stay pending. Only the final all-type checkpoint compares with no pending
  exclusion at all.
Terminal accounting at the final checkpoint: every resolved logical record is **applied**, **superseded** (an `order_by`
loser or a node/`FOLLOWS` record overwritten later) or **rejected** (malformed: node ones in the rejection file,
relationship ones terminal in the DLQ, never retried or parked). Never-enqueued and still-uncertain sends are reported in
their own buckets.
- Nodes: per key, fold valid records in (partition, offset) order; a non-null declared property overwrites, a null one
  never clears (node_loader.py PATCH semantics). Node writes do not use the DLQ, so redelivery after a restart replays in
  offset order and the fold stays exact through faults.
- `order_by` types: per identity (type, source label, source key, target label, target key), the record with the
  strictly greatest `order_by` value. Exact through faults and replay.
- `FOLLOWS` null-patch pairs that keep their preconditions: fold of their physical offsets in order with
  null-never-clears (`SET r +=`), exact. Complete pairs: the stored values must equal one complete record sent for that
  pair; differences from the last record are counted and reported as the documented B5 limit (retry-consumer
  resurrection memory is per process), not as failures.
- Physical duplicates (DLQ at-least-once, replays) are counted separately from logical records and never fail a check.

**Quiet point** (`soak/checker.py`):
1. Pause the generator; let already scheduled endpoint sends (at most 120 s) finish; resolve uncertain sends.
2. Snapshot end offsets of the **source** topic partitions (frozen: the generator is paused). Consumer groups checked:
   only the stable stream groups `<id>-<Label>`, `<id>-<type>` and `<id>-retry-consumer`. Bulk groups from the handover
   are run-scoped and excluded.
3. Drain until each node and edge group's committed offset reaches the source snapshot on every partition. A partition
   empty at the snapshot (low = high watermark) needs no commit; a non-empty one without a commit is not drained.
4. The DLQ frontier moves (a retry can re-publish an unresolved row past any snapshot and commit its old copy), so it
   is followed, not frozen: read DLQ end offsets, then the retry consumer's commits, then the end offsets again; drained
   only when, on every DLQ partition, the commit equals the end (or the partition is empty, low = high watermark, with
   no commit, matching the product's own low-watermark rule in retry_consumer.py) and both end reads agree, on two such
   bracketed reads at least 30 s apart, with the parked topics' ends unchanged between them. DLQ backlog means this
   unresolved lag, not retained topic depth.
5. Deadline: 120 s + one retry window + the calibrated catch-up bound. Missing it is red, with the lagging partitions.
6. Freeze `FOLLOWS` eligibility (above), then compare Neo4j to the model with paginated reads (no row cap), datetimes
   normalised to UTC instants and dates to ISO calendar dates: counts per
   label and type, a per-key property digest, and the first 20 missing, extra and different keys. Also: no duplicate node
   keys, no two relationships of one type between one identity, no relationship to a node the model lacks.

**Supervisor** (`soak/supervisor.py`): runs the installed `graph-loader start --mode stream --config ... --run-id ...` the
way production would: on exit it runs the narrowed recovery, waits a backoff, and starts the same command again; it
records every exit code, reason line and restart. A restart not caused by an injected fault is a failure.

**Faults** (`soak/faults.py`), on a schedule, each with its expected outcome (B7, B8, B10):

| Fault | Expected outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Neo4j stopped 30 s, later 6 minutes | fleet stays up; `stage=write-outage` and recovery lines; `graph_loader_write_outage_seconds` rises then returns to 0; no restart |
| SIGTERM to the CLI during a Neo4j outage | the supervising CLI exits 0; loaders log `stage=edge-drain-abandoned` (or node) with `reason=neo4j_outage` and exit 0, or an edge loader is killed after Docker's 10 s grace (exit 137, documented); nothing committed for held batches; restart after Neo4j returns redelivers them; model checks hold |
| Kafka broker restarted (stop 30 s, start) | not defined by the docs: record what happens. Pass = the model's checks hold at the next quiet point; any fleet exit is recovered by the supervisor |
| One edge loader killed | fleet exits 1 (B8); supervisor restart; checks hold |
| Retry consumer killed | fleet exits 1 (B8); supervisor restart; checks hold |
| One node loader killed | fleet exits 1 (B8); supervisor restart; checks hold |
| Lease server killed | supervised restart in place, no fleet exit |
| Graceful SIGTERM, then start with `PURCHASED` replicas 2 -> 3 | exit 0; resumes from committed offsets; no re-read of committed records |
| `kill -9` of the CLI | containers keep running; narrowed recovery; restart; checks hold |
| Burst (5x for 3 minutes) | lag rises and drains within the calibrated catch-up bound |

**Continuous sampling** (every 15 s, JSON lines): whitelisted group lag, DLQ backlog, parked counts by `rid`, the
write-outage gauge (metrics enabled for the soak's CLI and containers), container RSS and restarts (owned containers
only), host `MemAvailable` and Docker filesystem free space, and the lock-wait detector with its positive control at the
start of every fleet incarnation. The query log is read incrementally across rotation and Neo4j restarts with bounded
memory. A sampling gap over 60 s, or a query-log segment lost to rotation before it was read, is a **coverage failure**,
never counted as zero waits.

## Pass criteria (S2 and S3)

1. At every quiet point (pending identities excluded) and at every reconciliation checkpoint (nothing excluded): exact
   for nodes, `order_by` types and null-patch `FOLLOWS` pairs that kept their preconditions; the permitted set for
   complete `FOLLOWS` pairs, with the B5 count reported. Inconclusive keys (uncertain sends, null-patch pairs that lost
   their preconditions) are listed; more than 0.1% inconclusive keys is a harness failure. Null-patch coverage: eligible,
   compared and inconclusive pair counts are reported per window; a run with zero strictly compared null-patch pairs is a
   coverage failure.
2. Terminal accounting at the final checkpoint: every resolved logical record is applied, superseded or rejected; none
   unaccounted; no new parked copy after the final replay. Every cohort `rid` parks, then is applied after its
   checkpoint. Ordinary parking is reported per window as a capacity outcome with its cause (burst, outage, node
   backlog).
3. Zero Neo4j lock waits on loader writes, with full coverage and the positive control detected in every incarnation.
4. Each fault produces its expected outcome; no fleet exit or container restart without an injected cause.
5. Lag returns to 0 within the catch-up bound fixed in calibration (default: 2x the burst length + 5 minutes) after each
   burst and each recovered fault.
6. No OOM kill and no breach of the memory or disk guard. RSS is reported per container incarnation after a 10-minute
   warm-up, next to DLQ backlog and graph cardinality; growth that does not level off is reported for the orchestrator
   to classify (expected cache or backlog growth vs a leak), not failed automatically. A container above 2x its S1
   measured peak is red.
7. Cleanup by recorded ID; foreign snapshot unchanged on the host.

### Story E13.F1.S2 — Calibration, bulk handover and a 30-minute compressed run

- Status: planned; estimate: 1 engineering day(s).
- Story dependencies: E13.F1.S1.
- Evidence: `../evidence/E13.F1.S2.md`, run directory under `var/e13-soak-<date>/`.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. Calibration: measure sustained stream throughput with the full schema (records/s at which whitelisted lag stays flat
   for 10 minutes, after a 5-minute warm-up). Record base rate = 50% of it and the catch-up bound. Calibration does not
   prove 8-hour feasibility; S3 does.
2. Handover: generate a baseline (default 50,000 customers, 10,000 products, 20 stores, 300,000 relationships) with all
   endpoints first, load it with `graph-loader start --mode bulk`, check it exactly, then start the stream with the same
